# Clinical claims to verify

The clinical claims and judgements paper 2's protocol depends on, for anyone checking them against the literature and for clinicians. Each item states the claim with its numbers and sources, where the protocol uses it, and what a reviewer should answer. Citations in [brackets] resolve to entries in [CITATIONS.md](CITATIONS.md); [citation needed] marks a source not yet in it. Values we chose are marked **[assumption]**; "our arithmetic" marks a value derived from cited numbers. "D*n*" is a decision in DECISIONS.md, and "SPIKE §*n*" a section of the harness record, [SPIKE_2026-10-28.md](development/SPIKE_2026-10-28.md). Code that depends on an item carries `# CLINICAL-REVIEW: CRn` ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) rule 3). CR1-CR10 follow the order of the study lead's clinical list, as [DECISIONS.md](reference/DECISIONS.md) numbers them; CR11-CR20 are the further clinical claims the protocol rests on. The items were drafted on 2026-10-28 by an AI agent (Claude, Anthropic) under authority delegated by the project lead, Pranav Reddy, and await his review.

**Status on 2026-10-28.** No item is ticked. No clinician has reviewed any item, and the team has no clinician. Cohen Ferrari's literature fact-check has not started, and it is a literature check, never clinical review.

**Two sign-offs.** Each item has two, filled in by a maintainer:
- **Literature checked (name, date):** the claim matches its cited sources. Filled in as `Literature checked: <name>, <YYYY-MM-DD>.`
- **Clinician reviewed (name, role, date):** a clinician agrees with the claim. Filled in as `Clinician reviewed: <name>, <role>, <YYYY-MM-DD>.`

A factual item is ticked once its literature check is in. Items marked **Needs a clinician** are clinical judgments a literature check cannot settle (CR1-CR10, CR17, CR18); they are ticked only when both sign-offs are in. A test must reject a ticked sign-off without its names and date, a filled-in one that is not ticked, and an item ticked without the sign-offs it needs; it is a requirement before `protocol-v1` and is not yet written.

**How to review an item.** Read each cited source and compare it with the numbers in the item. Record the sign-off in the format above. If a claim is wrong or a judgement is not shared, say what would replace it and why; a change to the protocol is a dated amendment in [DECISIONS.md](reference/DECISIONS.md) before anything it affects is computed. *Added here:* where paper 1's [CLINICAL_REVIEW.md](https://github.com/pkarakala/ghost-lows/blob/main/docs/CLINICAL_REVIEW.md) has the same claim (named in the item), a sign-off recorded there may be copied here with its date and a link, if the claim is unchanged.

**Freeze.** *Added here:* open items do not block `protocol-v1`. The defaults stated here apply until a reviewer's answer leads to an amendment, and every results text lists the items still open.

- [ ] **CR2. The virtual person: wizard, rescue rule and night response.** (a) *Wizard.* At meal start the person boluses carbs / CR + max(0, (CGM − target) / ISF) − max(0, IOB), floored at 0 and rounded to 0.1 U, with the profile's CR, ISF and target (100 mg/dL), paper 1's carb miscounting (SD 20%) and missed boluses (p = 0.05 per meal), reading the displayed CGM **[assumption]**. IOB is oref0's latest value in closed loop and the bolus IOB in open loop. How real bolus calculators apply IOB is not yet sourced; [CITATIONS.md](CITATIONS.md) lists Zisser 2008 as a candidate source that has not been read, so it is not cited here. (b) *Rescue.* 15 g of fast carbohydrate when the displayed CGM is below 70 mg/dL by day, or at or below 55 mg/dL at night (23:00-07:00, when only the urgent-low alarm is assumed to wake the person); recheck after 15 min; repeat while below 70 mg/dL; the carbohydrate is eaten but not entered, so the controller is not told [CDC 2024; Garg 2022] **[assumption]**. The two triggers are paper 1's threshold alarm (70 mg/dL) and its ungated urgent-low floor (55 mg/dL) [Reddy 2026]. (c) *Night response.* The person always wakes to an urgent-low alarm (probability 1.0, paper 1's assumption) **[assumption]**; the results also give the compression effect at wake probabilities q = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1, derived from the pathway arms ([RESEARCH.md](reference/RESEARCH.md) §3). One reference point: LoopEval's donor-calibrated alarm-wake chance is N(0.72, 0.2) [LoopEval 2026]. (d) *No other corrections.* Outside meals the person gives no correction bolus, as in paper 1, except in the correcting-person arms (CR4) **[assumption]**. Where: D5; SIMULATION.md (the person). Paper 1's CR17 covers the rescue rule with the fault-free CGM as its trigger. Questions: is this wizard a fair picture of AID users' bolus calculators, in particular subtracting IOB from the whole bolus rather than from the correction only? Do AID users treat at below 70 mg/dL by day, and wake at night only for the urgent-low alarm? Is leaving rescue carbohydrate unentered typical?

- [ ] **CR7. Whether 12 h captures the harm.** Each arm runs 12 h from fault onset **[assumption: covers a compression low (median 45 min [Facchinetti 2016]), the controller's response, the rebound and the next meal]**. Faults held for the whole window (drift, gain errors) are scored per 12 h of exposure. The small interference level is still active at the window's end: its hold ends at 10.5 h, and with a 3-h decay constant about 61% of its +20 mg/dL peak remains at 12 h (our arithmetic); the large level keeps about 8% (our arithmetic). The time to re-converge (the first time after the fault ends when the true-glucose difference stays under 5 mg/dL for 60 min **[assumption]**) shows how many events had not settled by the window's end (BENCHMARK.md). Where: D2. Questions: does 12 h capture the rebound after a night compression low and its effect on the following morning? Is anything clinically important likely to happen later?

- [ ] **CR16.
