## 2. oref0 bridge (node/bridge.js)

- One long-lived, stateless JSON-lines worker; Python sends the whole state each call. Ops: `step`
  (glucose-get-last, lib/iob, lib/meal, determine_basal), `profile` (lib/profile, wired as
  bin/oref0-get-profile.js:217-244), the single stages `glucose_status`, `iob`, `meal`,
  `determine_basal`, plus `ping` and `exit`.
- Each stage is wired as its CLI (file:line in the code) and gets its own fresh copy of the inputs,
  because oref0 mutates them (lib/iob/history.js:269-270 adds `timestamp`, glucose-get-last.js:48
  averages into the newest entry, cob.js:143-144 rewrites `clock` and `current_basal`). The IOB and
  meal results pass through a JSON round trip, as they do through files in the CLI. The CLI-only
  checks of bin/oref0-meal.js are kept (carb ratio < 3, lines 88-92; fewer than 36 readings, lines
  131-135).
- SMB: the library is called directly, so microBolusAllowed is honoured (the CLI turns it off without
  an `autotune` directory, bin/oref0-determine-basal.js:105-114).
- stderr: console.error/log/warn/info and process.stderr.write are dropped (returned in `log` with
  `debug: true`); rT.reason is kept. The bridge writes replies through a saved stdout handle.
- Runs with TZ=UTC (the Python client sets it).

### 3.2 Ledger (src/ghostlows_aid/ledger.py)

Nightscout treatments, as Loop/AAPS upload them and as oref0's history.js reads them:
`Temp Basal` (rate U/h, duration min, created_at), `Meal Bolus` (insulin, carbs), `Correction
Bolus` (insulin; SMBs carry `isSMB`). A temp that is superseded or cancelled gets its actual
duration; one replaced in its first minute is dropped. Sent newest first (history.js:274-280
drops a record newer than the one before it). Temps are rounded as oref0's lib/round-basal.js
(0.05 U/h below 10 U/h, `profile.model` unset; checked against node on 21 rates); wizard boluses to
0.1 U (ASSUMPTION: pump bolus increment, oref0's own default); SMBs are already in 0.1 U steps from
determine-basal.

oref0's lib/meal gets the ledger as pump history and an empty carb history: meal/history.js:85
reads the carbs of `Meal Bolus` records. Giving the ledger as both would count every meal twice.

### 3.3 Loop (src/ghostlows_aid/loop.py)

Paper 1's minute loop on T1DPatient (ghostlows engine.py:150-185), reused pieces by import (patient
tables, Quest, scenario, Breton noise, device range 40-400, validity floor 10, HypoTreatment, seeds,
IOB curve). Order within a minute: truth recorded; on a 5-min sample the CGM is read (noise, the
fault hook on `isf + noise`, the device display), the person may treat a low, the controller runs;
then meals are eaten and bolused; then the pump delivers the minute's basal or temp, boluses and
SMB. determine-basal runs every 5 simulated minutes, also when its reading is missing (it then sees
the last reading's age).

- Controllers `oref0_tb`, `oref1_smb` (microBolusAllowed true, SMB with COB and UAM) and
  `open_loop` (paper 1's fixed basal), all with the same person and fault hook.
- CGM display: clipped to 40-400 and rounded to whole mg/dL (ASSUMPTION: Dexcom/Nightscout `sgv`
  are integers; `cgm_integer=False` keeps paper 1's unrounded display).
- Seeds: a seed has paper 1's meal, perturbation and noise seeds for the patient (task_seeds), so
  meals and sensor noise equal paper 1's; the person's own draws use
  `SeedSequence([seed, patient index, 7])` and are keyed by sample index.
- Speed: T1DPatient parameters as a SimpleNamespace instead of a pandas Series (the model reads them
  by attribute): bitwise identical, 14x faster (0.56 s per simulated day for the ODE).
- Per-sample log: truth and interstitial glucose, CGM noise, cgm_clean, cgm_observed, fault type
  and id, what the controller and the person were shown and what was sent, the gate flag, glucose
  status (glucose, delta, short and long avgdelta, date), IOB (total, basal, bolus, activity), COB
  and meal carbs, eventualBG, insulinReq, the rT rate, duration, units and reason, the temp set,
  insulin delivered (basal, bolus, SMB), carbs (meal, rescue), and per-stage bridge times.

## 4. Online faults (src/ghostlows_aid/faults_online.py)

- `FaultSchedule.draw(seed, days, config)` draws every instance of paper 1's suite exactly as
  `apply_run` does (same per-type streams, same draw order, thinning by severity, the labels'
  priority), from (seed, planned run length, FaultConfig) only; `FaultSchedule.for_run(suite_seed,
  key, days, config)` is the schedule `apply_faults` gives the run `key` = (patient_id, seed, mix).
  `apply(k, raw_k)` corrupts reading k (raw = isf + noise, before the device range) and returns
  (value or NaN, fault_type, fault_id). The loop calls it on every sample.
- Operators needing future samples: none. Drift, PISA and dropout are functions of time; spike
  noise is pre-drawn per sample from the instance's stream; stale holds the first reading of its
  segment (a past value). The realisation does depend on the planned run length (`days` sets the
  number of candidates, their start times and the complete nights), so a schedule is drawn once for
  the whole planned run, never extended. A schedule may be switched on late (a branch) except
  inside a stale segment, which raises.
- `SingleFault`: one event of a type at onset t0. `draw(kind, onset, seed)` takes its shape from
  paper 1's distributions (PISA depth and duration: two-piece log-normals, Facchinetti 2016;
  dropout: exponential, 90% under 20 min; frozen 10-30 min and spike 15-30 min uniform; spike SD 20
  x severity), from its own stream `SeedSequence([seed, 11, kind])`. `at_quantile(kind, onset, q)`
  sets each size at quantile q (q = 0.5 gives the median PISA, 24 mg/dL for 45 min; q = 0.95 gives
  48 mg/dL for 70 min). Any parameter can be set explicitly. The levels of PI_decisions rev 2 §6:
  - dropout, G6 long-gap model (`gap_model="g6"`): 1 + continuations with probability 0.855 up
    to the third sample and 0.727 after (Vettoretti 2023 Model 4; the wiring is the memo's
    ASSUMPTION): median 20 min, 73% at least 15 min;
  - drift (typical): paper 1's end-of-life size U(10%, 20%) x severity at a uniformly drawn sensor
    age, sign set by the arm, held for the window as a constant gain (ASSUMPTION, memo §6 #10);
  - gain (severe drift +/-20%, stress +/-40%): a held multiplicative gain, explicit only;
  - bias (interference): additive, rising as g(u) = (u/tp) exp(1 - u/tp) to the peak at tp, held
    to `hold_until_min`, then decaying exponentially; small = +20 mg/dL, tp 2.5 h, held to 10.5 h
    (three doses 4 h apart), tau 3 h; large = +60 mg/dL, tp 2 h, no hold, tau 4 h (22% left at
    8 h). Sizes and timing from FDA K193371 and Maahs 2015 as the memo cites them; hold and decay
    are ASSUMPTIONs. A negative size gives a sustained false low. The event "ends" when the bias
    is down to 10% of its peak (ASSUMPTION, as paper 1's PISA END_FRACTION).
  `describe()` gives the realised parameters for the per-arm log.

Tests (tests/test_faults_online.py, CGM traces only): each fault type alone, the mixed suite at
moderate severity and a dense mixed suite (all types, severity 1, PISA every night, 3-day sensors),
at seeds 0, 1, 7 and 90001, on a 7-day trace that crosses both device limits: `cgm_observed`,
`fault_type` and `fault_id` equal `apply_run` sample by sample within 1e-9 (measured: bitwise equal,
55,702 of 55,702 readings) and the instance tables agree; `apply_faults` over two runs is reproduced
run by run with `for_run`; changing readings after sample j changes no output up to j; a late start
equals the tail of a full run; the loop's own hook on the loop's own clean readings equals
`apply_run` post hoc, and with nobody shown the faulted signal truth equals the fault-free run.
Single events: nothing changes before onset; PISA equals paper 1's `from_size` attenuation;
dropout, frozen and spike windows; the G6 gap model's median (20 min) and continuation
probabilities; the interference shape at both levels (peak, hold, decay, 22% at 8 h); a negative
bias reads low; gains and held drift are multiplicative; drawn sizes follow paper 1's medians.

## 5. Branching episodes (src/ghostlows_aid/episodes.py)

The design of PI_decisions.md revision 2 (§2, §6, §7), which supersedes the brief where they
differ.

- Trunk: one fault-free run of a (controller, patient, seed) from 00:00 on day 1 for 67 h, on
  paper 1's default mix (scenario drawn for 3 days); the first 24 h are warm-up.
- Slots, minutes from 00:00 day 1: night 2820-3300 (23:00 day 2 to 07:00 day 3), dose 1860-2340
  (07:00-15:00 day 2), any 1440-2880 (day 2). One onset t0 per slot on a 5-min boundary, from the
  stream (seed, patient, 12, slot), stratified: seed k of N draws t0 uniformly among the 5-min
  boundaries of the k-th of N equal sub-intervals. No controller in the key.
- Snapshot (ClosedLoop.snapshot) at each t0 as the trunk passes it: a deep copy of the whole loop
  state (body: state vector, time and every T1DPatient attribute; the sensor-noise generator; the
  1-min arrays; the logged samples and events; the pump ledger and the running temp; the
  controller's and the person's reading histories; the rescue timer and the person's last bolus;
  oref0's last IOB; the fault and detector objects). The scipy dopri5 integrator is not copied: it
  is rebuilt from (y, t), which continues bit for bit. oref0 holds no state (stateless bridge).
- `ClosedLoop.branch(snapshot, faults, routing, person, gate)` restores and sets one arm. A gate's
  detector is first fed every reading the controller was shown before t0 (it has watched from the
  start; it acts only from t0, so the pre-t0 history stays the trunk's).
- Arm: restore, set, run W = 12 h. The fault-free arm of a slot is the trunk's own continuation.
- Arm catalogue (`ARMS`), PI_decisions §6: 17 fault arms for every controller (tier 1: PISA
  typical, short dropout, frozen typical, spikes typical, drift low and high typical, interference
  large) and 17 variant arms for oref0_tb (pathway 4, Baysal gate 4 parameter sets x {clean night,
  PISA typical} = 8, matched filter 2, fakecgm on frozen severe 1, correcting person 2): 34 arms for
  oref0_tb, 17 for oref1_smb and for open_loop. A fault's shape is drawn from (seed, patient, 13,
  fault number), so every controller and every variant arm of that fault meets the same event.
- `run_unit` runs a trunk, snapshots its three onsets, and yields the clean arm of each slot and then
  each branched arm lazily. `arm_trace` cuts one arm to [t0, t0 + W] with everything PI_decisions
  §8 asks to log: 1-min truth; 1-min insulin split into basal or temp, the person's boluses and
  SMBs; the 5-min displayed CGM, the person's and the controller's input, what was sent, the gate
  flag and oref0's outputs and reason; events; IOB and COB at onset from the trunk; the realised
  fault (`SingleFault.describe`). Outcomes are computed by the analysis package, not here.

Random numbers (which draws can diverge between arms: none):

| Draw | Source | Key | Same in every arm because |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meals, carb-count errors, missed boluses, skipped meals, sensitivity, exercise | paper 1's make_scenario | paper 1's meal and perturbation seeds | drawn before the run |
| Sensor noise | simglucose CGMNoise | paper 1's noise seed | one draw per 5-min sample, drawn whether the reading is faulted, missing, gated or shown; state in the snapshot |
| Night response | the person | (person seed, 7, sample index) | keyed by sample, not by how many draws came before |
| Onsets | episodes.onset | (seed, patient, 12, slot) | no controller or arm in the key |
| Fault shape | SingleFault.draw | (seed, patient, 13, fault number) | shared by controllers and variant arms |
| Spike noise | SingleFault | (event seed, sample index) | keyed by sample |

What diverges after t0 is only what the shared numbers are used for: whether a rescue is taken,
what a wizard dose comes to, what oref0 decides.

Tests (tests/test_branching.py; faulted arms are compared on CGM streams, meals and actions
only): (a) for each controller, a branched clean arm equals the continuous run bit for bit (truth,
1-min insulin split, every logged sample, events, ledger), restored into the same and into a fresh

Detectors (src/ghostlows_aid/detectors.py; tests/test_detectors.py): paper 1's Baysal rules as an

## 6. Routing hooks and the harness-validity suite (tests/test_mechanisms.py)

Hooks (all off by default; `Routing`, `Person`, `ClosedLoop.branch`):
- pathway: `controller_sees` and `person_sees`, each "faulted" or "clean" (the 2x2 of PI_decisions
  §6 with the clean arm);
- detector gate: a detector between the displayed CGM and the controller; policy "hold" re-sends
  the last unflagged reading with a fresh timestamp, "drop" sends nothing; without `gate=True` an
  attached detector only observes (flags logged). The person always sees the raw displayed CGM;
- guard ablations, deliberate manipulations of oref0's inputs, never of its code: device
  `fakecgm` (skips the flat-CGM check, determine-basal.js:177-185) and `restamp_missing` (re-sends
  the last reading stamped "now" when one is missing, which bypasses the stale-data stop, :187);
- the correcting person (PI_decisions §5): a correction of max(0, (CGM - 100)/ISF - IOB), rounded
  to 0.1 U, when the displayed CGM is at or above 250 mg/dL between 07:00 and 23:00 and the person
  gave no bolus in the last 3 h (SMBs do not count: ASSUMPTION).

The suite (oref0_tb unless stated; adult#001, calibration seed 90001, default mix, rule1800; each
check asserts on controller inputs, reasons and actions only):

| Check | Result |
|---|---|

| Restamp ablation on the same gap | no stale reason; the re-sent value turns flat, and the flat check takes over |
| Flat-CGM check on a 120-min freeze, real device name | every cycle from 50 min into the freeze is "CGM data is unchanged" |
| Same freeze with device `fakecgm` | no flat reason; entries carry `fakecgm` from t0 only |

## 10. Grid run script (experiments/run_grid.py)

## 12. Deviations from TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md

1. §2: oref0 is not vendored under `vendor/oref0/`; it is installed from npm into node/node_modules
   from the committed node/package-lock.json (`npm ci --ignore-scripts`). The lockfile also holds
   oref0's full declared dependency tree (request, share2nightscout-bridge, ...), but the bridge
   requires only lib/ modules, which need lodash, moment and moment-timezone.
2. §1.1/§2: one more wall-clock read than the design lists (cob.js:26 via lib/meal/total.js), handled
   as in §2.1 above.
3. §6: the profile is generated by oref0's own lib/profile from pump settings, so the scheduled basal
   oref0 sees is rounded to 0.001 U/h (lib/profile/index.js current_basal via basal.js:29), while the
   pump delivers paper 1's exact basal. Difference at most 0.0005 U/h.
4. §5.2: the CGM shows whole mg/dL by default (paper 1 does not round); an option restores paper 1.
5. §5.3: wizard boluses are rounded to 0.1 U (paper 1 does not round); an option restores paper 1.
6. Branching test (d) of the brief asks that arms share `cgm_clean` after t0. `cgm_clean` is the
   interstitial glucose of each arm's own body plus the shared noise, so it is shared only until
   the arms' insulin or rescue carbohydrate first differs; the test checks exactly that, and that
   the noise draws and the meal log are shared throughout.
7. PI_decisions §5, correcting person: the dose is (CGM − target)/ISF − IOB with IOB floored at 0
   and the dose floored at 0, so a negative IOB (after a suspend) never adds insulin.
8. PI_decisions §5, wizard: carbs/CR + max(0, (CGM − target)/ISF) − max(0, IOB), floored at 0
   (TECHNICAL_DESIGN §5.3); the memo writes the correction term without its floor.
9. PI_decisions §4, calibration: a patient's runs on the three calibration seeds are pooled
   before the criteria are applied, and the criteria are scored over all 3 days, the first one
   included (oref0 starts without history). A patient meeting the criteria at no ISF scale keeps
   1.0. The tie-break "larger ISF" was not needed (§7).
10. PI_decisions §7, timing: arms were timed with their faults off; fault-specific costs (fewer
    readings in a dropout, a stale-data exit) are not in the projection, nor is the analysis step
    (0.03-0.04 s per arm).
11. The brief's calibration grid asked for both ISF rules under both oref modes; the memo selects on
    `oref0_tb` only, and the scale step was run under `oref0_tb` only.
