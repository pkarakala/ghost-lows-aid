# Citations

Every source the repository cites, for anyone adding or checking a claim. Every claim in the docs, code comments and results cites a key from this file. Add new sources here first (and to `references.bib`), then cite them.

In code, write `# SOURCE: <key>`. Separate several keys with `;`; a key may be followed by a locator, as in `# SOURCE: Vettoretti 2023 (Model 4)`. Keys are "Author YEAR" (with a, b suffixes where two works collide); software, standards, regulatory documents and web pages use a short descriptive key. A key is an identifier: its year is the year the first citing document used, and it never changes once cited. Keys from the research notes, the study lead's memo and paper 1 that differ from the keys here are listed in the last table.

Every entry has a BibTeX record in [references.bib](references.bib) under the same key with spaces and punctuation removed (`Mensh 2013` is `Mensh2013`). For an entry with a DOI the record holds the full author list, title, journal or conference, year, volume, issue and pages of the DOI registry's record (Crossref; DataCite for Zenodo records), and the record's note says which registry and when; the Reference column here shows the first three authors only. Where a registry splits a compound surname or misspells a name, the record uses the correct form and its note says so. The record's year is that of the issue or volume; the key keeps the year the first citing document used (Eichenlaub 2025 and Niu 2025 appeared online before their issue). Web pages, repositories, standards and FDA documents are `@misc` records with a URL and the access date 2026-10-28. Titles are the registry's; this file writes an em dash in a title as a colon.

**Status column**
- **opened**: abstract or full text was opened during this research, by a research agent or by the citations writer; the bracket says what was opened and by whom. Web pages and PDFs were read through a text-extraction tool on 2026-10-28; a quotation is as that tool returned it and is compared with the page before it goes into a paper.
- **ref-only**: seen in reference lists, search snippets, secondary summaries or a metadata record only; not opened.
- **verify**: details, numbers or the DOI must be checked before the source goes into a paper; the bracket says what.

**Used for** gives the numbers the protocol takes from the source, as the source or the research note states them. A conversion that is not in the source is marked as the research note's arithmetic. Interference and recall entries describe what a label, recall or report says; none is a statement about how safe a commercial system is. The research notes R1, R4 and F2 are internal: cite the underlying source, never the note.
