## 1. Definitions

| Term | Definition used in this project | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **True glucose** | The simulated body's plasma glucose at 1-min resolution. Every harm outcome is scored on it. | [simglucose; Kovatchev 2009]; D1 |
| **Displayed CGM** | The 5-min CGM reading the virtual person sees: paper 1's sensor (Johnson noise, device range 40-400 mg/dL, shown in whole mg/dL, SPIKE §7), with the arm's fault applied. | [Breton 2008; Reddy 2026]; D1 |
| **Controller input** | The CGM reading the controller receives. It equals the displayed CGM except in the pathway and gate arms. | This project; D7 |

| **Arm** | One branch from a trunk at t0: a fault type and level, or none, and optionally a pathway, gate, guard or person variant. It runs for W = 12 h **[assumption, §2]**. | D2, D7 |
| **Fault-free arm (clean continuation)** | The trunk itself over [t0, t0 + 12 h]. It costs no extra simulation. | D2 |
| **Branching counterfactual (episode)** | The design in which every arm restarts from a full-state snapshot of the trunk at t0 (body, controller, pump and random-number state). The arms share their whole history before t0 and, by common random numbers, all exogenous randomness after it (meals, announcement errors, missed boluses, sensor noise, exercise), so they differ only by the fault and the responses to it. | This project; D2 |
| **Common random numbers** | Shared random draws across arms, keyed by event or time so they stay aligned when the arms' trajectories differ. | This project; D2 |
| **Effect (τ, θ)** | τ_a(p, r) = Y_a(p, r) − Y_clean(p, r) for outcome Y; patient effect τ̄_a(p) = mean of τ_a(p, r) over valid seeds; population effect θ_a(c) = mean of τ̄_a(p) over the patients. | D2; §2 |

| **IOB, COB** | Insulin on board and carbohydrate on board, as oref0 computes them from the delivered insulin and the announced carbohydrate; in open loop, IOB is the bolus IOB on the same insulin curve (SPIKE §3.4). | [OpenAPS docs] |
| **SMB, UAM** | oref1's super micro-boluses (small automatic boluses) and its detection of unannounced meals. Both are on in `oref1_smb` and off in `oref0_tb`. | [OpenAPS docs]; D3 |

## 2. Questions

### Primary question

> **When an unmodified open-source automated insulin delivery (AID) algorithm acts on a faulty CGM reading, how much harm does one fault event cause, on true glucose, compared with the identical situation without the fault; which faults cause the most; and when, by whom and through which mechanism is the harm done?**

**Estimand.** For arm a, controller c, patient p and seed r, the individual effect is τ_a(p, r) = Y_a(p, r) − Y_clean(p, r), where Y is an outcome over the window [t0, t0 + 12 h], scored on true glucose or on insulin and carbohydrate delivered, and Y_clean is the fault-free arm from the same snapshot. The patient effect τ̄_a(p) is the mean over the patient's valid seeds. The population estimand is θ_a(c) = (1/P_a) Σ_p τ̄_a(p): the mean over the P_a virtual patients with at least one valid seed for arm a, with equal weight per patient (P_a = 30, 10 children, 10 adolescents and 10 adults, when no patient is lost; BENCHMARK.md §1, §4.1). The patient is the unit of analysis; seeds only reduce within-patient noise ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) rule 8). A patient with no valid seed for an arm is left out of that arm's estimate, and the count is reported (D11). The window W = 12 h **[assumption: covers a compression low (median 45 min [Facchinetti 2016]), the controller's response, the rebound and the next meal; clinical review CR7]**. The simulation is reported in ADEMP form [Morris 2019]: aims as in this section; data-generating mechanism in SIMULATION.md; estimands as above; methods in BENCHMARK.md; performance measures are the Monte Carlo standard errors and CI widths of the estimates, because this is not a comparison of statistical methods.

The primary question is answered by the confirmatory hypotheses H1 and H2 (§3), by the per-arm effects on every outcome for every fault and controller (secondary, descriptive) and by the mechanism and dose-response analyses (secondary).

### The four-way rule (H1 and H2)

The test, the Holm procedure and the rule are defined once, in [BENCHMARK.md §4.3](BENCHMARK.md#43-the-confirmatory-family-h1-h2) (`hypotheses.bootstrap_p`, `hypotheses.holm`, `hypotheses.decide`); this summary does not replace it. H1 and H2 form one family, tested by Holm's procedure at familywise α = 0.05 [Holm 1979] on bootstrap p-values from B = 10,000 replicates: the smaller p-value is rejected when it is at or below 0.025, and the larger only then, when it is at or below 0.05 (ties: H1 first). Each hypothesis then takes exactly one of these readings, in this order:

1. **Not estimable**: the estimate or a bound of its 90% CI is not finite.
2. **Meaningful harm**: Holm rejects θ_k = 0, θ̂_k > 0 and θ̂_k ≥ S_k. Its CI is given at the Holm-adjusted level (97.5% at step 1, 95% at step 2).
3. **Opposite direction**: Holm rejects θ_k = 0, θ̂_k < 0 and θ̂_k ≤ −S_k. It is reported as an unexpected direction, never as harm or as a benefit of the fault.
4. **Negligible**: the 90% CI lies strictly inside (−S_k, +S_k): two one-sided tests at α = 0.05 each [Lakens 2017], not adjusted for multiplicity.
5. **Inconclusive**: otherwise; the text gives the CI and the direction of the estimate.

The words a result may be given are in BENCHMARK.md §7. A well-measured null for H1 is a result: the benchmark is the contribution whichever way H1, H2 and C1 come out.

## 4. What is new, and why

**Prior work, by theme.**

- **Fault size and frequency.** Compression lows have a median depth of 24 mg/dL (5th-95th percentile 10-48 mg/dL) and a median duration of 45 min (30-70 min) on the Dexcom G4 [Facchinetti 2016], and are thought to follow reduced local blood flow under pressure [Mensh 2013]. Published compression rates differ about tenfold: 0.03 per person-day [LoopEval 2026], 0.321 per day reaching a CGM below 85 mg/dL [Lobo 2026] and about 0.33 per day [Idi 2025] **[clinical review CR13]**. G6 trial data give about 0.13 (adults) and 0.24 (children) gaps per day, typically 25-30 min long and up to about 95 and 120 min [Vettoretti 2023] **[clinical review CR15]**. Drug interference raises CGM readings: +9.3 mg/dL after 1000 mg vitamin C and 18.4 and 19.7 mg/dL after a second and third dose on the Libre 2 [FDA K193371]; +61 mg/dL at 120 min after 1 g acetaminophen on the Dexcom G4 [Maahs 2015] (numbers from a secondary report until the letter is read); +3.1 mg/dL on the G6 [Calhoun 2018]; +55 to +114 mg/dL after intravenous acetaminophen [Matsuyama 2025] **[clinical review CR9]**.
- **Real-world harm through dosing.** Compression lows can cause undesirable pump shut-offs in predictive low-glucose suspend (PLGS), as shown on PLGS trial data (over 1,125 nights; about 3% of overnight readings marked as compression) [Baysal 2014]. In one child on a hybrid closed loop, compression false lows led to suspension and rebound highs, and 12 of 14 nights needed intervention [Altnickel 2026]. Manufacturer labels name insulin over-delivery from false highs [Medtronic Guardian 4 UG; FDA K203774], and two Class I recalls of Libre 3 sensors (false highs in 2024, false lows in 2025) published no magnitude or time course [FDA 2024 Libre 3 false-high recall; FDA 2025 Libre 3 false-low alert]. This evidence is recall-, label- and single-case-level; no cohort quantifies AID harm from CGM faults **[clinical review CR14, CR19]**.
- **CGM errors injected into closed loops.** A research model predictive controller was tested against calibration errors and real night-time attenuations [Wilinska 2009]; a PID stand-in for OpenAPS against generic software faults [APS_TestBed 2022]; a fuzzy controller in simglucose against gaps [Lu 2020]; a reinforcement-learning controller against sensor manipulation [Chang 2024]; Loop and an oref port on their own simulated plants against a 45-min false low [LoopEval 2026]; and oref0 0.7.1 in simglucose against one false-high attack [Bird 2026]. Detection studies added faults without measuring harm [Yu 2018; Feng 2018].
- **oref run in simulators.** AndroidAPS configurations in the UVA/Padova simulator [Toffanin 2020]; oref re-implemented in MATLAB with T1DMS [Schmitzer 2022]; oref0 0.7.1 on digital twins [Somers 2025]; Trio's oref JavaScript against its Swift port in simglucose [Screven 2026]. None tested sensor faults.
- **Paired and registered simglucose studies.** Shared seeds across arms and registered protocols are routine in 2026 [Agha 2026; Banjar 2026]. Digital-twin what-if replays of real AID data exist [Kovatchev 2025].
- **Compression detectors.** Real-time rule-based detection on PLGS data [Baysal 2014]; a prospective onset detector [Lobo 2026]; retrospective detectors [Idi 2023; Manzoni 2025; Pelagalli 2025]. All are scored on detection metrics. UVA patents claim restarting insulin after a compression false low, without results [US 2024/0139415; US 2024/0138770]. General hazard monitors are scored by mitigation success in closed loop [Zhou 2021; KnowSafe 2025].
- **Guards and ablation.** Single safety rules of a PLGS system were removed by replay [Cameron 2014]. oref0's noise rules and AndroidAPS's smoothing shipped without a published evaluation [oref0 PR 833; AAPS smoothing docs].
- **The person pathway.** A Padova decision simulator models a person's boluses, corrections and hypotreatments driven by CGM error [US 12,354,751]; user errors were compared with and without an artificial pancreas [Cester 2025].
- **Linking layers.** The same forecasters were scored on alerts and on dosing [Namazi 2026]; CGM accuracy was linked to insulin-dosing outcomes in silico [Kovatchev 2015]. Paper 1 measured the alarm layer under this fault suite [Reddy 2026].

## 5. Scope

**In scope:** simulation in simglucose 0.2.11 (the 2008 UVA/Padova model) with its 30 virtual patients, through paper 1's engine imported from `ghostlows` 0.4.0; OpenAPS oref0 0.7.1 run unmodified, temp basals only (`oref0_tb`, primary) and with SMB and UAM (`oref1_smb`, secondary); paper 1's open loop as the reference for the alarm layer and the person pathway; the 17 fault arms and 17 variant arms of D6 and D7; harm on true glucose; the alarm layer on the displayed CGM through paper 1's alarms; the released harness, harness-validity suite and analysis code.

**Out of scope for `protocol-v1`:** Trio's oref, Loop and other algorithms (an amendment may add one, DECISIONS.md, open items); commercial systems and any statement about their real-world safety; real-data estimates of harm; prospective data collection; treatment advice or any change to real devices; concurrent faults, except in the additivity runs; oref0's noise rules (no noise field is sent); regulatory use.

## 6. Known limitations to state up front

Every results text copies these.

- Virtual patients come from the 2008 UVA/Padova model, without counter-regulation; hypoglycaemia after over-delivery may be deeper and longer than in people, and rebound sizes are model-bound [Dalla Man 2014; Visentin 2018] **[clinical review CR16]**.
- One open-source algorithm family (oref0 0.7.1 and its SMB mode), one target (100 mg/dL) and settings we derived; nothing transfers to commercial systems **[clinical review CR5]**.
- Fault shapes and rates come from literature models and paper 1's assumptions; real fault mixes vary across sensors and people. Paper 1's short-gap, frozen-value and spike rates have no source; the long-gap model comes from retrospective G6 trial downloads, which may miss short real-time gaps that the sensor later backfilled [Vettoretti 2023] **[clinical review CR15]**.
- Interference sizes rest on few studies, one read only through a secondary report [Maahs 2015]; decay times are assumptions; the ±40% gain errors are stress levels, not measured sizes **[clinical review CR8, CR9]**.
- A scripted virtual person: wizard boluses, rescue on the displayed CGM, always waking for urgent lows at night, no corrections outside meals (except the correcting-person arms) **[clinical review CR2, CR3, CR4]**.
- Single fault events in 12-h windows; interactions between faults are checked only by the additivity runs **[clinical review CR7]**.
- One smooth sensor-noise model (paper 1's limitation); no noise field is sent, so oref0's noise rules are not exercised.
- No real-data validation of harm.

*Added here*, from the evidence already in hand, also to be copied:

- Confidence intervals describe this cohort of 30 virtual patients, not a population of people.
- Five of the 30 patients (adolescent#002, adolescent#007, child#002, child#003, child#008) do not meet every calibration acceptance criterion under any allowed ISF scale; they stay in the cohort at scale 1.0, flagged `outside_acceptance`, because patients are never dropped (AGENTS.md rule 8). Four of them fail on time above range, which an ISF scale cannot fix. Sensitivity analysis S2 repeats H1, H2 and C1 without them ([D17](DECISIONS.md); SIMULATION.md §2.3, §2.5) **[clinical review CR5]**.
- In clean harness runs oref0's flat-CGM branch fires on 3.6-5.6% of loop cycles, because whole-mg/dL readings with smooth noise often give a change of 0 mg/dL (SPIKE §3.6). The clean arm therefore already contains some flat-CGM cycles; the guard contrasts are differences on top of them.
- The second detector (paper 1's matched filter) uses the same compression model that generates the injected artifacts, so it has a home advantage that real artifacts would not give it (paper 1's limitation).

## 7. Credibility

Framed by ASME V&V 40-2018 and the FDA's 2023 guidance on computational modelling [ASME 2018; FDA 2023].

- **Question of interest.** The paired, per-event glycaemic harm of CGM fault types when unmodified oref0 acts on them, in silico.
- **Context of use.** Research: ranking faults and mechanisms to guide where fault handling and detection matter for open-source AID, and generating hypotheses for clinical and real-data study. Not for regulatory submissions, treatment decisions or claims about commercial systems.
- **Model influence:** high, because simulation is the only evidence. **Decision consequence:** low, because the use is research. **Model risk:** moderate, so the credibility evidence below is proportionate to it [ASME 2018; FDA 2023].
- **Verification evidence.** oref0's own tests; bridge parity with the shipped command-line tools and with 52 determine-basal fixtures (oref0's 42 test calls plus stale, flat, `fakecgm`, noise, maxDelta and low-suspend cases); independence from the wall clock; online fault injection equal to paper 1's post-hoc injection; determinism; bit-identical pre-onset histories and identical post-onset random streams across arms; a branched clean arm equal to the continuous run; causality and truth-isolation tests. Status on 2026-10-28 (SPIKE §2.2, §4, §5): the 52 fixtures give oref0's own result through the bridge, oref0's 84 mocha tests pass, replies do not change when the wall clock moves to 2019 or 2034, online injection equals post-hoc injection on 55,702 of 55,702 readings, and for each controller a branched clean arm equals the continuous run bit for bit.
- **Harness-validity suite (released; N12).** In closed-loop scenarios: the stale-data stop acts during a long gap; the flat-CGM check acts on a long freeze with a real device name and not with `fakecgm`; max_iob is set and binds; SMB is blocked by maxDelta on a jump; a zero temp follows a predicted low. Status on 2026-10-28: each check passes for adult#001 on calibration seed 90001 (SPIKE §6); the full suite runs again on the frozen code.
- **Validation evidence available.** Clean closed-loop outcomes compared with outcomes in people using open-source AID that runs the OpenAPS algorithm: the CREATE randomised trial of AndroidAPS with oref1 [Burnside 2022], and any peer-reviewed OpenAPS outcome report the citations writer verifies **[clinical review CR17]**. Controller behaviour under faults against oref0's documented logic (a zero temp on a predicted low; the stale-data stop) [OpenAPS docs]. Qualitative agreement with real-world reports: pump suspensions attributed to compression artifacts in a PLGS trial [Baysal 2014], suspension followed by rebound highs under AID in one case [Altnickel 2026], and manufacturer labels that name insulin over-delivery from false highs [Medtronic Guardian 4 UG; FDA K203774]. Real-world evidence of AID harm from CGM faults is recall-, label- and single-case-level only. No quantitative validation of fault harm exists or is claimed.
- **Applicability.** The 2008 model lacks counter-regulation and intraday variability [Dalla Man 2014; Visentin 2018]. The FDA's acceptance of the UVA/Padova simulator for preclinical testing concerns the commercial T1DMS versions, not simglucose [Kovatchev 2009].
